# ISO 27001 / 27002 Gate

## Purpose

Use this gate to determine whether a proposed architecture decision or code change aligns with the organization’s information security management and control expectations under ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 27002.

This gate is about disciplined controls, documentation, accountability, and operational readiness.

## Core principle

A change is not acceptable merely because it works technically.
It must also fit the platform’s control environment.

## Mandatory pass criteria

### 1. Policy and control alignment
- [ ] The change does not contradict existing security policy.
- [ ] Any required policy, standard, or runbook updates are identified.
- [ ] Security exceptions are documented and approved.

### 2. Access control
- [ ] Access to the changed component follows least privilege.
- [ ] Administrative or privileged access is restricted and reviewable.
- [ ] Shared credentials are avoided or explicitly controlled.

### 3. Asset and information handling
- [ ] Information handled by the change is classified or at least understood.
- [ ] Sensitive information has handling rules.
- [ ] Retention, deletion, and audit implications are considered where relevant.

### 4. Secure operations
- [ ] Operational procedures for the change are documented when needed.
- [ ] Deployment, configuration, and maintenance responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Logging, backup, restore, and support implications are understood.

### 5. Supplier and dependency control
- [ ] New vendors, hosted services, or material third-party dependencies are reviewed.
- [ ] Security ownership of the dependency is understood.
- [ ] Exit or replacement implications are considered for critical dependencies.

### 6. Change management
- [ ] The change is documented before implementation.
- [ ] The change is reviewed and approved at the right level.
- [ ] The change can be tested before production use.
- [ ] The production rollout is controlled.

### 7. Incident and continuity impact
- [ ] Security incident implications are understood.
- [ ] Business continuity or resilience implications are understood.
- [ ] The change does not create an unsupported single point of failure without approval.

### 8. Auditability
- [ ] Records exist to show what changed, who approved it, and what was tested.
- [ ] The design and implementation decisions are traceable.
- [ ] Control evidence can be produced later for audit or review.

## Required design questions

1. What policy or control does this change rely on?
2. What records will prove the change was reviewed and approved?
3. What operational procedure needs to change because of this?
4. What dependency, supplier, or hosting risk does this introduce?
5. What evidence would an auditor ask for later?

## Evidence required

A passing review should include:
- design record or ADR
- approval record
- test evidence
- operational note or runbook update where needed
- dependency review where needed
- traceable change record

## Fail conditions

Block the change if any of the following are true:
- no evidence trail exists
- operational ownership is unclear
- privileged access implications are undocumented
- critical dependency risk is unreviewed
- the change cannot be explained or evidenced to an auditor later
