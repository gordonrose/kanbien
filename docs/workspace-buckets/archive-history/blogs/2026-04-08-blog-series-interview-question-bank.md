# Blog Series Interview Question Bank

## Purpose

These questions are meant to help turn the series into something personal,
specific, and believable. They should draw out your lived experience rather
than produce generic AI-development commentary.

Use these in small batches while drafting each post.

## Post 1. Vibe Coding Is Real, But It Lies About Progress

- What did AI-assisted building feel like the first time it really clicked for
  you?
- What kinds of progress became suddenly easier?
- What did you initially mistake for progress that later turned out to be
  incomplete?
- When did you first notice that code was appearing faster than trust was
  forming?
- Was there a moment where you thought, “this is impressive, but I’m not sure
  I can rely on it yet”?

## Post 2. The Moment I Realized AI Needed Guardrails

- What concrete moment made the problem obvious rather than theoretical?
- Which skipped step bothered you most after the fact?
- What kind of change felt “small” at first but turned out to need real
  process discipline?
- What did that teach you about shared seams, security, or architectural drift?
- How did your own thinking change after that moment?

## Post 3. The Guardrails That Changed Everything

- Which guardrail gave you the biggest improvement in confidence?
- Which guardrail felt annoying at first but later proved valuable?
- What repo/process change most reduced the feeling of AI output being
  slippery or hard to trust?
- Which artifact or skill made future work noticeably easier?
- What changed in your pace once those guardrails existed?

## Post 4. Building A SaaS Platform At The Speed Of Refinement And Design

- What does “develop at the speed of refinement and design” mean to you in
  plain language?
- Which parts of engineering now feel more leverage-heavy than they did before?
- How has your role changed when implementation is less of the bottleneck?
- How did the repo’s sequencing shape that understanding?
- What order of platform work now feels obviously correct to you in hindsight?

## Post 5. What I’d Tell Anyone Starting A SaaS Platform With AI Today

- What would you tell someone to build first?
- What would you tell them not to rush?
- What trap would you warn them about most strongly?
- Which guardrails are essential from day one, and which can come slightly
  later?
- What does a “healthy” AI-assisted startup engineering loop look like to you?

## Cross-Series Questions

- What do you believe now that you did not believe when you started?
- Where do you still feel uncertainty or caution?
- What part of your approach would skeptics misunderstand if they only saw the
  final repo?
- What trade-off are you consciously making that is worth defending?
- What sentence best captures your current philosophy on AI-assisted
  development?

## Strong Candidate Pull Quotes

These are the kinds of statements worth listening for in your answers:

- “AI made it possible to move faster, but not automatically safer.”
- “The turning point was when I stopped measuring progress by generated code.”
- “The real bottleneck moved from implementation to judgment.”
- “Guardrails didn’t reduce speed. They made speed useful.”
- “The best part of AI-assisted building is not output volume. It’s faster
  refinement.”
